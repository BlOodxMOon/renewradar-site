Streaming, music, cloud storage, news, fitness, software, games, delivery memberships: almost everything is a subscription now. Each one is reasonable on its own. Together they can create a constant background feeling of paying for too much. That is subscription fatigue.

## The signs

![Eight signs of subscription fatigue](fig:a)

If three or more of these sound familiar, it is worth spending an evening on a reset.

### 1. You cannot name them all

Try listing your subscriptions from memory. Then check Google Play, your email and your statements. The gap between the two lists is the problem.

### 2. Unrecognised charges

A charge you do not recognise is often an old trial or an app subscription under a different company name.

### 3. Overlap

Two music services, three cloud storage plans or several streaming platforms with similar content.

### 4. Unused apps

If you have not opened an app in a month, ask whether you would sign up again today.

### 5. Forgotten trials

If a trial has turned into a charge before, it will happen again without a system.

### 6. A surprising yearly total

Monthly costs feel small; the yearly total does not.

### 7. Paying for one thing

Keeping an entire plan for one show or one feature can be fine, but it should be a conscious choice.

### 8. Avoiding your statement

If checking your account makes you uneasy, that is fatigue talking.

## A calm reset

![A one-evening reset](fig:b)

The aim is not to cancel everything. Most people keep the services they truly enjoy and drop a few they forgot about. That is usually enough to make a real difference.

## Staying out of fatigue

After a reset, the hard part is not slipping back. Subscriptions creep back one trial at a time. Two habits prevent that:

- **Track every new subscription and trial the day you start it.**
- **Get a reminder before each charge**, so every renewal is a small decision instead of a surprise.

RenewRadar, the subscription tracker app for Android, is designed for exactly this. Your monthly and yearly totals stay visible at the top of Home, free trials have their own countdown, and reminders arrive before you pay. When something is no longer worth it, cancel it and mark it as cancelled, so your list stays honest.

Subscription fatigue is not about how many services you have. It is about paying for things you did not choose to keep. Making every renewal a choice is the cure.

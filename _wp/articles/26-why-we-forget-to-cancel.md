If you have ever found a charge for something you stopped using months ago, you are in very good company. Forgotten subscriptions are one of the most common money leaks there is. That is not because people are careless. It is because subscriptions are built in a way that makes forgetting easy.

## The reasons we forget

![Why cancelling slips our minds](fig:a)

### Doing nothing means paying

Subscriptions continue until you act. Your attention is needed to stop paying, not to keep paying. Anything that relies on attention eventually fails.

### Small amounts do not trigger alarms

A charge of a few dollars blends into a statement. It is not large enough to make you stop and ask what it is.

### The decision moment is far away

You sign up when you are interested. The trial ends or the plan renews weeks or months later, when you are thinking about something else entirely.

### Cancelling takes effort

Some services hide the cancel option or show offers before letting you leave. When cancelling feels like a chore, it gets postponed, and postponed again.

### Optimism

"I will use it more next month" is one of the most expensive sentences in personal finance. We tend to imagine our future selves as more active, more organised and more interested than we really are.

## Why willpower is not the answer

Trying harder to remember does not scale. With a handful of subscriptions on different cycles, the number of dates to remember quickly becomes unrealistic. What works is a system that remembers for you and asks the question at the right time.

## Habits that work

![Habits that work better than memory](fig:b)

1. **Capture subscriptions immediately.** The best time to record a subscription is the minute you start it. Later, you will not remember the exact date or price.
2. **Let something else do the remembering.** A reminder before each charge turns a forgotten renewal into a conscious choice.
3. **Look at the yearly total regularly.** Monthly costs feel small. Yearly totals make waste visible.
4. **Act when you decide.** When a reminder arrives and you know you do not want the service, cancel right then, not "later today".

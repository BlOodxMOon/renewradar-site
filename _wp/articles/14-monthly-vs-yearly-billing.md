Most subscriptions offer a choice: pay every month, or pay for a year up front at a lower average price. The yearly option usually saves between 15 and 40 percent. Whether it saves you money depends on one thing: how long you keep using the service.

## Compare in the same unit

Prices in different cycles are hard to compare directly. Convert everything into a monthly cost:

- **Weekly:** price x 52 / 12.
- **Quarterly:** price / 3.
- **Yearly:** price / 12.

![Different billing cycles converted to a monthly cost](fig:b)

This also reveals surprises. Weekly plans, common in mobile apps, are often the most expensive way to pay.

## The trade-offs

![Monthly and yearly billing compared](fig:a)

### When monthly is better

- You are still deciding whether you like the service.
- You use it for a season, such as a sports streaming service or a fitness app in the new year.
- You like to rotate services, such as watching one streaming service for a month and then switching.
- Your budget is tight and a large one-time charge would hurt.

### When yearly is better

- You have used the service for months and use it often.
- The yearly discount is large enough that you break even well within a year.
- You want to protect yourself from price increases during the year.

## The hidden cost of yearly plans

Yearly plans are cheaper per month but easier to forget. A monthly charge reminds you that a subscription exists twelve times a year. A yearly charge appears once, often as a surprise.

That is why yearly renewals deserve earlier reminders. In RenewRadar, a good setting for yearly plans is a reminder 14 days before and another 3 days before, enough time to check whether you still use it and to compare prices.

## A simple rule

Start monthly. Switch to yearly once you are sure you will use the service for most of the year, and track the yearly renewal date from the day you switch.

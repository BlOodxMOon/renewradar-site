Streaming, music, cloud and software services raise their prices from time to time. Each increase is usually small: a dollar or two a month. But spread across several subscriptions, small increases change your yearly total noticeably.

![A small increase over a year](fig:a)

## How companies announce price changes

- **Email**, often with a subject line that does not mention the price.
- **In-app notices** that appear when you open the app.
- **Google Play or Apple notifications** for subscriptions billed through the app stores. For some increases, the store asks you to accept the new price before it applies.
- **Your statement**, which is the moment many people first notice.

Most services announce changes some time before they take effect, which gives you a window to decide.

## How to spot increases early

1. **Search your email** every few months for "price", "pricing" and "changes to your plan".
2. **Read app store notices** carefully rather than tapping "OK".
3. **Compare your statement** with the price you expect. A subscription tracker makes this easy, because you know what each charge should be.

## What to do when a price goes up

![Steps to respond to a price increase](fig:b)

You have more options than keeping or cancelling:

- **Switch to a cheaper tier**, such as an ad-supported plan or a plan with fewer screens.
- **Switch to yearly billing** if you use the service every day. The yearly plan may still be cheaper per month.
- **Share a family plan** if the service offers one.
- **Rotate** with other services instead of paying all year.
- **Cancel**, if the new price is more than the service is worth to you.

## A yearly check

Once a year, look through every subscription and ask:

- What do I pay now, and what did I pay a year ago?
- Is there a cheaper plan that still fits how I use it?
- Would I sign up again today at this price?

Price increases are normal. Paying them without noticing is optional.

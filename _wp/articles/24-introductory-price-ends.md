An introductory price is like a free trial that costs a little. You pay a reduced price for the first period, and then the subscription continues at the regular price. It is a common way for streaming, music and software services to win new customers.

## Why intro prices are easy to miss

- **The jump is automatic.** No new confirmation is needed when the price goes up.
- **The first charges are small,** so they barely register on your statement.
- **The end of the offer is a date, not an event.** Nothing happens that reminds you.
- **The full price can be much higher** than the intro price, sometimes ten times as much.

![An intro offer over one year](fig:a)

## Common types of intro offers

- **A low price for the first months**, such as three months for a dollar.
- **A discounted first year**, after which the plan renews at full price.
- **A bundle discount** that ends when one part of the bundle changes.
- **A student or promotional rate** that ends after a fixed time or when you no longer qualify.

## Before you accept an intro offer

1. Find the regular price and when it starts.
2. Check whether the regular plan is monthly or yearly.
3. Decide now whether you would pay the regular price. If not, plan to cancel before it starts.
4. Check whether you can cancel immediately and still keep the intro period. Many services allow it.

## How to track an intro offer

The key date is when the regular price starts. That is when you want a reminder.

In RenewRadar, the subscription tracker app for Android, add the subscription at the intro price, with the next renewal date. Write the regular price in the notes field, such as "Full price $11.99 from March 1". Then set a reminder a few days before the date when the full price begins.

![Steps to track an intro offer](fig:b)

When the regular price starts, edit the subscription and update the price. Your monthly and yearly totals update immediately, so you see the real cost from that day.

If the offer is a free period rather than a discounted one, use **Free trial** instead. RenewRadar then shows it in the Trials section with a countdown and the price after the trial.

## What to do when the reminder arrives

- **Still using it often?** Keep it, and update the price in the app.
- **Using it occasionally?** Look for a cheaper plan or a pause option.
- **Not using it?** Cancel before the full price starts.

Introductory prices are a good deal when you take them on purpose. A single reminder before the price jump keeps them that way.

If all your subscriptions are billed in one currency, tracking them is simple arithmetic. Once dollars, euros and your local currency are mixed together, it gets harder, and most apps quietly assume you only use one.

## Who runs into this

![People who pay in several currencies](fig:b)

A typical example: a designer in Manila pays for a music plan in pesos through Google Play, design software in US dollars on the vendor's website, and a cloud backup service in euros. Each charge arrives in a different currency and is converted by the bank at its own rate.

## The problem with converted totals

The obvious solution is to convert everything into one currency. It looks neat, but it has a catch: the exchange rate an app uses is not the rate your bank or card applies. Card networks and banks add their own margins and fees, and rates change daily. A converted total can look precise and still be several percent off.

For a budget, an honest number beats a precise-looking wrong one.

## Tips for multi-currency subscriptions

1. **Enter the currency you are billed in**, not the one you think in. That is the number your statement will show.
2. **Note the card or account** used for each subscription. Cards with foreign transaction fees make foreign-currency subscriptions more expensive.
3. **Check whether a local price exists.** Some services charge less when billed in local currency through Google Play than on their website in dollars.
4. **Review exchange-sensitive plans yearly.** A dollar-priced yearly plan can cost noticeably more in your currency after a big rate change.

## A quick comparison of approaches

- **Single-currency apps:** simple, but wrong for anyone billed in more than one currency.
- **Apps that convert totals:** one neat number, but based on a rate that may not match your bank.
- **Per-currency totals:** a little more to read, but every number is real.

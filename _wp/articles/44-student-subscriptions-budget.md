Students are a favourite audience for subscriptions: music, streaming, note-taking apps, language learning, cloud storage, design tools. Many offer discounts, but on a student budget even discounted plans add up, and a forgotten trial can hurt.

![A student subscription checklist](fig:a)

## 1. Use student discounts, and track when they end

Many music, streaming and software services offer student pricing. These discounts usually need re-verification every year and end when you graduate. When the discount ends, the subscription often continues at the full price.

Add the date your student status needs renewing, or when the discount ends, to your tracker so it does not catch you out.

## 2. Check what your school already gives you

Universities and colleges often provide office software, cloud storage, research tools and sometimes design or coding tools for free. Check before paying for your own.

## 3. Share plans

Family and duo plans can cut the cost per person a lot. Make sure everyone knows who pays and that people cancel their individual plans when they join.

## 4. Rotate entertainment

You do not need every streaming service at once. Pick one for the month, watch what you want, then switch.

## 5. Be careful with free trials

Free trials are tempting on a tight budget, but a forgotten trial is an unexpected charge. Track every trial the day you start it, with a reminder before it ends. If you only want the trial, cancel right after signing up when the service allows you to keep access until the end.

## 6. Avoid weekly app plans

Weekly plans in apps can cost several times more than monthly or yearly ones over time. Always check the monthly equivalent.

## The student rule of thumb

Before any new subscription, ask: would I pay for this at the full price after the discount or trial ends? If not, set a reminder, or skip it.

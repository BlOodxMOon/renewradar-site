Free trials let you use a paid service before deciding whether it is worth the money. That is useful. But the way most trials are set up means that doing nothing is the same as saying yes to a paid plan.

## Why a free trial needs your card

Most services ask for a payment method before the trial starts. On Android, that is often your Google Play payment method. On websites, it is usually a card or a digital wallet.

The reason is simple: the trial is the first period of a subscription. When it ends, the subscription continues and the first payment is taken automatically. No new confirmation is needed, because you agreed to it when you started the trial.

## Automatic renewal, in plain words

Auto-renewal means the subscription keeps going until you cancel it. Each period, whether a week, a month or a year, the service charges your payment method again.

A few details catch people out:

- **The first charge can be bigger than you expect.** Some trials convert to an annual plan, not a monthly one.
- **The end date is not always obvious.** Some trials end exactly 7 or 30 days after sign-up, others at the end of a billing day in another time zone.
- **Deleting the app does not cancel anything.** The subscription lives in your Google Play, Apple or website account, not in the app.
- **Cancelling usually keeps access until the end.** On Google Play, for example, you can typically cancel a trial right away and still use it until the trial ends.

## Why people forget

Nobody plans to forget a trial. It happens because the trial starts at a moment of interest and ends at a moment when you are thinking about something else. The confirmation email is buried, and the service has little reason to remind you loudly.

Starting two or three trials around the same time makes it harder still. Each has its own length and its own end date.

## How to use free trials without surprise charges

1. **Decide your plan when you sign up.** If you only want the trial, cancel right away when the service allows it. You usually keep access until the end.
2. **Write down the end date and the price after the trial.** Not just the monthly price, but whether it switches to yearly.
3. **Set a reminder a few days before the end.** One reminder is easy to dismiss. Two, such as three days and one day before, are much harder to miss.
4. **Know where you will cancel.** Google Play, the App Store or the website, depending on where you signed up.

Free trials are not a trick, but they are designed around the default of doing nothing. A countdown and a timely reminder change the default back to a real decision.

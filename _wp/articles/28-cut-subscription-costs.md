The fastest way to lower subscription costs is to cancel things. The smartest way is to keep paying for what you value and stop paying for everything else. Here are ten tactics that do that.

![Ten ways to pay less](fig:a)

## 1. Cancel what you do not use

Start with the obvious. Anything you have not used in a month, and would not sign up for again today, can go. Most people find at least one.

## 2. Rotate streaming services

Instead of paying for four streaming services all year, keep one or two and switch every month or two. Watch what you want, cancel, move to the next. Our guide to [subscription rotation](/guides/subscription-rotation/) explains how.

## 3. Share with a family or duo plan

Many music, video and storage services offer family plans that cost much less per person. Make sure you know who pays and track it, so it does not end up as two people paying for the same thing.

## 4. Switch to yearly, but only for daily use

Yearly plans are cheaper per month. They are only a saving if you use the service all year. Read our comparison of [monthly and yearly billing](/guides/monthly-vs-yearly-billing/).

## 5. Try an ad-supported tier

Several streaming services offer cheaper plans with ads. If you watch occasionally, the difference may not bother you.

## 6. Check bundles you already pay for

Phone carriers, internet providers, credit cards and other subscriptions sometimes include services. You may be paying separately for something you already have.

## 7. Use student or regional discounts

Students and some regions get significantly lower prices. Check eligibility before renewing.

## 8. Pay in your local currency

Some services cost less through Google Play in local currency than on their website in US dollars, and you avoid foreign transaction fees.

## 9. Avoid weekly plans

Weekly app plans often cost far more per month than monthly or yearly ones. A $2.99 weekly plan is about $13 a month.

## 10. Get a reminder before every renewal

Every tactic above depends on deciding before the charge. A reminder turns each renewal into a moment to ask: keep, switch or cancel?

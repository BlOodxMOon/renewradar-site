Creating an account has become such a normal part of installing an app that it is easy to forget it is optional. For many apps, an account exists mainly so the company can store your data, contact you or link your activity across devices.

RenewRadar, the subscription tracker app by Pixel DevCode, has no account at all.

## What "no account" means in practice

You install the app and open it. There is no sign-up screen, no email confirmation and no password. Your list starts empty, on your phone, and stays there.

![What no account means](fig:a)

## Why we built it this way

### Your list is nobody else's business

A list of subscriptions says a lot about you: what you watch, which apps you use for work, which news you read, even health or dating services. Without an account, that list is never connected to your name or email.

### Less to go wrong

Every account system is something that can be breached. Databases of emails and passwords are common targets. An app with no accounts has no such database.

### Faster to start

People abandon setups that take too long. With no sign-up, you can add your first subscription within a minute of installing.

## What about Pro?

Pro is a purchase through Google Play, so it is linked to your Google account, not to an account with us. RenewRadar checks with Google Play whether Pro was bought, using an anonymous app user ID. It does not learn your name or email.

## The trade-off: changing phones

Because there is no account and no cloud sync, your list does not automatically follow you to a new phone. We think that is the right trade-off for privacy, but it helps to plan ahead.

![Steps for changing phones](fig:b)

Pro users can export a CSV copy of the full list at any time. Free users with up to five subscriptions can re-add them in a few minutes.

## Will RenewRadar ever add accounts?

If we ever add optional sync between devices, it will be optional and clearly explained, and the app will keep working without it. The default will remain: no account, nothing leaving your phone.

## The bottom line

A subscription reminder app needs to know your subscriptions, not you. Leaving out the account keeps the app simple to start, safer to use and private by default.

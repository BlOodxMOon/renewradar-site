You open your banking app and see a charge for a service you only meant to try. The free trial ended, and the subscription started. Here is what to do, in order.

![What to do first](fig:a)

## 1. Find out who charged you

The charge description usually tells you:

- **"Google Play" or "GOOGLE *"** followed by the app name: billed through Google Play.
- **"Apple.com/bill"**: billed through the App Store.
- **The service's own name**: billed directly by the service, usually through its website.

Where you were charged is where you cancel and where you ask for a refund.

## 2. Cancel immediately

Cancel first, then ask for a refund. Cancelling stops the next charge, even if the refund is refused.

- **Google Play:** Play Store, profile picture, **Payments & subscriptions**, **Subscriptions**, choose the service, **Cancel subscription**.
- **Apple:** Settings, your name, **Subscriptions**.
- **Website:** your account page on the service, usually under Billing, Plan or Membership.

Our guide to [cancelling a free trial](/guides/cancel-free-trial-before-charged/) has more detail for each.

## 3. Ask for a refund

Refund rules depend on who billed you and where you live. Some general points:

- **Google Play:** you can request a refund from your Play order history or the Google Play help center. Requests made soon after a purchase have the best chance; after a short window, Google may refer you to the app's developer.
- **Apple:** use Apple's "Report a problem" page for the charge.
- **Direct billing:** contact the service's support. Many will refund a first charge if you have not used the service since the trial ended, especially if you ask quickly and politely.

Explain simply: you meant to cancel the trial, you have not used the paid period, and you have already cancelled. Refunds are not guaranteed, but they are often granted.

## 4. Check for annual charges

If the trial converted to a yearly plan, the charge may be large. Mention this in your refund request. Some services offer a partial refund or a switch to monthly billing.

## 5. Make sure it does not happen again

Most surprise charges come from the same pattern: a trial started in a moment of interest and forgotten afterwards. A few habits fix it:

- **Cancel straight away** if you only want the trial. On Google Play, you usually keep access until the trial ends.
- **Record the end date and the price after the trial** on the day you sign up.
- **Use two reminders**, a few days and one day before the end.

## Do not be too hard on yourself

Free trials are designed so that doing nothing leads to a charge. Forgetting one does not mean you are careless. It means you need a system, not a better memory.

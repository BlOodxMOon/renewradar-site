Ask someone how much they spend on subscriptions and they will usually name the two or three services they use every day. The real number is almost always higher. There is the cloud storage upgrade, the app bought for a single project, the news site, the yearly plan that renewed in spring.

Here is a simple way to find your real number in about ten minutes.

## Step 1: Collect every subscription

Check these places, in this order:

1. **Google Play:** open the Play Store, tap your profile picture, then **Payments & subscriptions** and **Subscriptions**.
2. **Apple:** if you have an iPhone or iPad, check **Settings**, your name, **Subscriptions**.
3. **Your email:** search for "receipt", "renewal", "subscription" and "trial".
4. **Your card and bank statements:** look at the last three months for repeating charges.
5. **PayPal or other wallets:** they keep a list of automatic payments.

Write down the name, price, billing cycle and next charge date of each one.

## Step 2: Turn everything into one monthly number

Subscriptions come in different cycles. To compare them, convert each to a monthly cost:

- **Weekly:** multiply by 52, then divide by 12.
- **Monthly:** use as is.
- **Quarterly:** divide by 3.
- **Yearly:** divide by 12.

A weekly plan at $2.99 sounds cheap, but it is about $12.96 a month. A yearly plan at $139 is about $11.58 a month.

## Step 3: Look at the yearly total

Monthly numbers feel small. Multiply your monthly total by 12 and the picture changes. This is the number that helps you decide what is worth keeping.

![A monthly total turned into a yearly total](fig:b)

## Step 4: Sort by value, not by price

Go through the list and ask one question for each item: would I sign up for this again today, at this price? Expensive services you use daily may be great value. Cheap ones you forgot about are pure waste.

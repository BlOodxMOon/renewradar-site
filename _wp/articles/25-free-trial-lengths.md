Some apps offer a three-day trial. Streaming services often give a week. Software tools like 14 days, and some services offer a month or more, especially around holidays. The length affects how much time you have to decide and how easy it is to forget.

## Common trial lengths

- **3 days:** common for mobile apps with weekly plans. Very short, and often followed by a weekly charge.
- **7 days:** common for streaming and music services.
- **14 days:** common for software and productivity tools.
- **30 days or one month:** common for bigger services and promotions.
- **Longer promotional trials:** two or three months, often tied to a device purchase, a phone carrier or a holiday deal.

## Shorter is not always safer

A three-day trial feels too short to forget. In practice, it is the easiest to miss, because the end arrives before you have built any habit around the app. And the plan after a short trial is often weekly, which costs more per month than most monthly plans.

## Longer is not always better

A 30-day or 90-day trial gives you more time, but the end date drifts far from the moment you signed up. By then, you may not remember signing up at all.

## Finding the exact end date

![Ways to find a trial's end date](fig:b)

The end date is usually the start date plus the trial length, but time zones and billing systems can shift it. Some services charge at the start of the last day. If you are unsure, treat the trial as ending one day earlier than you think.

On Google Play, the Subscriptions page shows the date the trial ends for trials billed through Play.

## When to set reminders

![Suggested reminders by trial length](fig:a)

The general rule: give yourself enough time to try the service properly, and at least one reminder the day before.

## Tracking trials in RenewRadar

In RenewRadar, switch on **Free trial** when you add a service, then enter the trial end date and the price after the trial. You can choose reminders of 14, 7, 3 or 1 day before, or on the day, for each trial separately.

Trials appear in their own section at the top of Home, sorted by which ends first, each with a countdown like "2 days left" and the price that will start afterwards. That way, a three-day app trial and a 30-day streaming trial sit side by side, in the right order.

## The best trial habit

If you already know you only want the trial, cancel right after signing up, when the service allows you to keep access until the end. Then the length does not matter at all.

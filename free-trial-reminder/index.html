Why free trials turn into charges

Free trials work because they need a payment method up front and renew on their own. The end date is usually 7, 14 or 30 days away, far enough to forget, and the confirmation email is easy to lose. By the time you notice, the first month or the whole year has already been charged.

Most services let you cancel during the trial and keep using it until the trial ends. The hard part is not cancelling. It is remembering to do it in time.

How trial reminders work in RenewRadar

When you add a subscription, turn on Free trial and enter two things: the day the trial ends and the price you will pay afterwards. From then on:

  • The trial sits in its own Trials section at the top of Home, sorted by end date.
  • Each trial shows a countdown, such as 2 days left, and the price after the trial.
  • Reminders count down to the trial end, not to a later renewal.
  • The trial is not added to your monthly total until it becomes a paid plan.

Reminders are notifications scheduled on your phone, so they arrive even if you have not opened RenewRadar in weeks, and they work offline.

Choosing the right reminder time

You can pick any mix of 14, 7, 3 and 1 day before, or the same day. The default is 3 days and 1 day before. A few rules of thumb:

Trial typeSuggested remindersWhy
7-day trial3 days and 1 day beforeEnough time to try it properly and still decide.
30-day trial7 days and 1 day beforeA week out is a good moment to ask whether you used it.
Trial that becomes a yearly plan14, 7 and 1 day beforeA yearly charge is large and harder to get refunded.
Service with a cancellation notice periodAdd the notice to your timingSome plans must be cancelled a few days before the end date.

When the trial ends

If you cancelled, mark the trial as cancelled in RenewRadar so it stops reminding you. It moves to the Cancelled list, where you can restore it later if you sign up again.

If you decided to keep it, edit the item, switch off Free trial, and it becomes a regular subscription with renewal reminders and a place in your totals.

A trial you have not touched stays at the top of Trials marked as ended, so it is hard to miss.

Tips for safer free trials

  • Add the trial the moment you sign up. You know the end date then; a week later you will not.
  • Check where you are billed. Trials started in an app are often billed by Google Play or the App Store, not by the service. Our cancellation guide shows where to cancel each one.
  • Cancel early if you can. Many services, including subscriptions billed through Google Play, let you keep access until the paid period would have started.
  • Note the price after the trial. Introductory prices often jump after the first period.
